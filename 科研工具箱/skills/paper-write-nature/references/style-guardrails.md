# Style Guardrails

Mechanical and stylistic checks for Nature-style academic writing.
Apply after section logic is correct (see SKILL.md for failure mode diagnosis).

---

## Academic Style

- Prefer cautious, precise prose over conversational confidence
- Avoid contractions in manuscript prose
- Avoid rhetorical questions
- Define abbreviations on first use
- Use British spelling for Nature-style prose (behaviour, colour, analyse)
- Figure legends: ≤ 300 words
- Titles: ≤ 75 characters including spaces

---

## Sentence Rules

- Each sentence ≤ 30 words
- If any sentence > 20 words, check for multiple propositions
- Split overloaded sentences rather than polishing cosmetically
- Last sentence of paragraph often becomes longest and weakest — check explicitly
- One core subject-verb proposition per sentence
- Active voice preferred for Nature

---

## Paragraph Rules

- Each paragraph: one controlling idea + support
- Support: data, comparison, explanation, consequence, literature, or limitation
- New idea = new paragraph (do not stack)
- Thematic linking, not repetitive openings
- Short paragraphs OK for Nature (3–5 sentences typical)

---

## Overclaim Checklist

Flag and soften these words:
- "prove" → "show", "demonstrate"
- "conclusively" → "strongly suggest"
- "unprecedented" → "to our knowledge, the first"
- "best" → "among the strongest", "competitive with"
- "superior" → "outperforms in [specific metric]"
- "first" → "to our knowledge, the first"
- "novel" → "new" or remove if clear from context

---

## De-AI Polish (mandatory check)

Remove or replace AI-typical language:
| AI word | Replacement |
|---------|-------------|
| delve | examine, investigate |
| pivotal | important, central |
| landscape | field, area |
| multifaceted | complex |
| underscores | shows, highlights |
| leveraging | using |
| groundbreaking | (remove or use evidence) |
| paradigm shift | (describe actual change) |
| in conclusion | (just state conclusion) |
| it is worth noting | (remove — just state it) |
| plays a crucial role | is important for |
| sheds light on | clarifies, reveals |

---

## Hedging Phrases

Appropriate hedging for scientific claims:
- "These results suggest that..."
- "A possible explanation is that..."
- "This may indicate..."
- "It is plausible that..."
- "One interpretation is that..."
- "To our knowledge,..."
- "Under the conditions tested,..."

---

## Transition Families

| Function | Phrases |
|----------|---------|
| Contrast | However, By contrast, Nevertheless, Conversely, On the other hand |
| Addition | Moreover, Furthermore, In addition, Additionally |
| Cause/Result | Consequently, As a result, Therefore, Thus, Hence |
| Concession | Although..., Despite..., Notwithstanding, While... |
| Sequence | First, Subsequently, Finally, Following this |
| Example | For instance, Specifically, In particular |
| Summary | In summary, Taken together, Overall |

---

## Articles (common errors)

- First mention singular count noun: `a` or `an`
- Later mention same item: `the`
- Generic plural: usually no article
- Unique entity: often `the`
- Abstract nouns used generally: often no article
- Bad: "The hypoxia induces..." → Better: "Hypoxia induces..."

---

## Numbers and Units

- Use numerals for measurements: `25 cm`, `3.2 s`
- Space between value and unit
- En dashes for ranges: `5–10 mg`
- Consistent statistical notation throughout

---

## Integrity Rules

- Do not invent references
- Do not alter quantitative values
- Do not upgrade association to causation
- Do not imply broader generalizability than study supports
- Do not let AI draft core scientific argument from scratch

---

## Results vs Discussion Syntax

Results verbs: was detected, increased, showed, enabled, achieved
Discussion verbs: may reflect, suggests that, could indicate, is likely due to, may facilitate

Do not let Results drift into Discussion syntax unless intentional.
