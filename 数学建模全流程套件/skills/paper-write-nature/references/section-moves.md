# Section Moves

Section-specific move patterns and phrase families for Nature-style writing.
Use after the section's rhetorical job is clear (see SKILL.md for section responsibilities).

---

## Introduction Moves

**Move order**: establish importance → summarize known → identify gap → state aim → indicate value

Phrase families:
- "Recent years have seen increasing interest in..."
- "X is a central issue in..."
- "Previous studies have shown that..."
- "However, the mechanisms underlying... remain poorly understood."
- "Few studies have examined..."
- "Here, we investigate whether..."
- "This work provides..."

Avoid: long historical throat-clearing, detailed results, inflated novelty before gap is defined.

Nature-specific: keep Introduction to ~600–800 words. Get to the gap fast.

---

## Results Moves

**Move order**: orient to figure/table → state main observation → quantitative detail → note patterns → brief comparison if clarifying

Phrase families:
- "Figure 1 shows..."
- "As shown in Table 1,..."
- "The most notable finding was that..."
- "Contrary to expectations,..."
- "No significant difference was observed in..."
- "These results are consistent with..."
- "In contrast to earlier reports,..."

Avoid: discussion-length mechanism explanations, repeating every visual detail from figure.

Nature-specific: each Results paragraph should be tied to a specific panel or table. Active voice: "We observed..." not "It was observed that..."

---

## Discussion Moves

**Move order**: restate main finding → plausible explanations → compare with earlier work → limitations → implications → future work

Phrase families:
- "Taken together, these findings suggest that..."
- "A possible explanation is that..."
- "This discrepancy may reflect..."
- "These results should be interpreted with caution because..."
- "An implication of this is that..."
- "Further work is needed to determine whether..."

Avoid: repeating Results in new words, claiming mechanism when only association shown.

Nature-specific: Discussion can be combined with Results in some Nature formats. Keep it focused — 3–4 paragraphs maximum for a Nature Article.

---

## Methods Moves

**Move order**: design/cohort → materials/data source → procedure → outcome measures → analysis/statistics → ethics

Phrase families:
- "A cross-sectional study was undertaken to..."
- "Samples were collected from..."
- "X was quantified using..."
- "We used... to assess..."
- "Differences were analysed using..."
- "All analyses were performed in..."

Avoid: "under standard conditions", "using routine methods", "data were analysed statistically"

Nature-specific: Methods often appear after Discussion. Be thorough — reproducibility is paramount.

---

## Abstract Moves

**Move order**: broad context → concrete gap → approach → key result with numbers → implication

Phrase families:
- "X remains challenging because..."
- "Here, we..."
- "Using..., we found that..."
- "We show that..."
- "These findings suggest..."

Nature-specific: 150–200 words maximum. Every word must earn its place. If a detail does not affect editorial triage, it does not belong.

---

## Title Moves

Target properties: searchable, specific, restrained, defensible.

Patterns:
- `[Core entity] in/through/by [mechanism or context]`
- `[Process] shapes [outcome] in [system]`
- `[Signature/pattern/framework] of [phenomenon]`

Nature-specific: ≤ 75 characters. No "A study of...", no vague hooks, no unverified "first".

---

## Conclusion Moves (if separate from Discussion)

**Move order**: return to aim → summarize decisive finding → state contribution → give boundary/forward look

Phrase families:
- "This study set out to..."
- "The present findings indicate that..."
- "These results extend our understanding of..."
- "Notwithstanding these limitations,..."
- "Further studies are required to..."

Avoid: introducing new experiments, ending on vague praise.
